# Landing and pricing pages

Rules for single-offer landing pages and pricing pages. Read with `anti-slop.md` (these pages attract the default section formula more than any other) and `persuasion-psychology.md` (reciprocity, defaults, anchoring).

## 1. Landing pages

**Rule:** One offer, one audience, one primary action. A landing page is not a homepage.

**Before designing, establish** (ask only if genuinely missing, otherwise state assumptions):
- the one primary action and what counts as a conversion
- the offer: exactly what the visitor gets
- the audience and the problem they arrived with
- the top three objections stopping them today
- traffic source and what visitors already know when they land
- what real proof exists: testimonials, numbers, case studies, logos with permission, screenshots, guarantee terms

**Apply:**
- **Match the source.** Visitors from an ad or email should see the same promise, wording and visual tone in the hero that brought them.
- **Above the fold:** outcome-led headline, a one or two sentence subheading saying what it is and who it's for, one primary CTA (verb + what they get: "Book a free survey", not "Learn more" or "Submit"), one real proof signal, and the real product or service shown.
- **Sections come from the objections, not a template.** Order the page by what this visitor needs answered to act. That may be three sections or twelve; it is rarely the stock hero/benefits/how it works/testimonials/FAQ/CTA sequence by default.
- **Proof sits next to the claim it supports**, not collected at the bottom.
- **Specific over general:** "Cut weekly reporting from 4 hours to 15 minutes" beats "Save time and streamline".
- **Risk reversal only if real:** free trial, no card required, cancel anytime, money-back guarantee. Statutory rights (e.g. 14-day online cancellation) are not a selling point.
- One primary CTA repeated, not several competing ones. Secondary actions are visibly secondary.

**Choosing the form:** classic hero + sections when a screenshot explains the product; long-form story when the offer needs education or faces scepticism; a minimal page for high-intent traffic (existing customers, email) and simple offers.

## 2. Pricing pages

**Rule:** The job isn't showing prices; it's removing uncertainty so the visitor can choose.

**Before designing, establish:** the value metric (seats, usage, projects), plans with monthly and annual prices, the 3-6 limits that actually matter per plan, the upgrade trigger, top objections, and whether there's a free plan or trial.

**Apply:**
- Three plans at most unless there's a strong reason; two is fine for simple products.
- Each plan: name, two-line description (who it's for, what it unlocks), price with unit and cadence ("£29 per user / month, billed annually"), 3-6 key limits and inclusions, one CTA.
- CTA verbs consistent across plans and matched to what happens next: "Start free trial", "Buy Pro", "Contact sales". Don't mix "Get started", "Try now" and "Sign up".
- A recommended plan only when it genuinely suits most buyers, and its highlight must reflect a real difference in content, not just a glow or gradient. Styled in the page's own shape and colour language.
- Monthly/annual toggle with the real saving stated ("Annual · save 2 months"). "Starting at" only when pricing truly varies.
- **All-in prices.** Any mandatory fee (setup, platform, per-booking) is included in the price shown, not added at checkout. Drip pricing is a banned practice under the DMCC Act.
- Full comparison below the cards, grouped by theme (Core, Collaboration, Admin and security, Support), highlighting what changes between tiers; linked from a "Compare plans" link.
- Usage-based pricing: a slider or calculator with obvious maths and the median customer as the default.
- Mobile: plan cards stack; comparison tables become per-plan lists or scroll inside their own container, never the whole page.
- FAQ answers the real blockers: cancelling, hitting limits, refunds, what counts as a seat, VAT, data and security.
- Never hide everything behind "Contact sales" when self-serve prices exist.

## 3. SEO for both

- Ad-only or time-limited landing pages: `noindex`, so they don't compete with evergreen pages.
- Evergreen landing and pricing pages: clear title and meta description, clean URL (`/pricing`), internal links from the homepage and relevant service or feature pages.
- FAQs written as plain question-and-answer text help search and AI answer engines. FAQ structured data is fine to add, but Google has shown FAQ rich results only for a narrow set of authoritative government and health sites since 2023, so don't promise clients rich snippets from it.
